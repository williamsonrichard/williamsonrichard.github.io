Miscellaneous

Song recordings

For some years, I have occasionally recorded (simply and unedited, via a mobile phone) various songs, accompanying myself on a guitar or keyboard. There are now around 100 recordings. A full list can be found here, but some examples are as follows.

  • Folk songs. Examples: Little Musgrave, Hughie the Graeme, and Jock o' Braidislee (ancient British ballads); Little Maggie, Man of constant sorrow, and The lakes of Pontchartrain (American folk/roots songs); Roving blade, Maggie Lauder, and A man's a man for a' that (Scottish and Irish folk songs).
  • British singer-songwriter material. Examples: Run Johnny run (Ralph McTell), Three hours (Nick Drake), and 1952 Vincent Black Lightning (Richard Thompson).
  • Bob Dylan, or songs I became aware of via Bob Dylan's performance of them. Examples: Mississippi, Waitin' for you, Not dark yet, Mutineer, Never let me go, Simple twist of fate, Les temps changent, and Bob Dylan's dream.
  • Bluesy material. Examples: Muleskinner blues (Jimmie Rodgers), Freight train (Elizabeth Cotton), Weary blues from waitin' (Hank Williams), and Folsom prison blues (Johnny Cash).
  • Original material (settings of my own poems or other people's to music). Examples: At midnight and still light, Waves etched, Disturbance, and Come bring with a noise ('Ceremonies for Christmas'). The first three of these are my own; the last is based on a poem by Robert Herrick.
  • Miscellaneous material: On every street (Dire Straits), Have you ever seen the rain? (Creedence Clearwater Revival), This nearly was mine (Rodgers and Hammerstein), Home (Cat Stevens), A most peculiar man (Paul Simon), Moon river (Audrey Hepburn; Mancini and Mercer), and La marche nuptiale (Georges Brassens).

Running

I picked up running at about the age of 34, and began to compete in 2019. I have gradually improved my times, and hopefully am not quite done yet!

  • 10000m (May 2026): 33:34
  • 5000m (August 2026): 16:01
  • Half-marathon (September 2026): 1:13:34

I have won a few races outright here and there, my favourite of which was Saldilten, near Namsos, in 2026.

My times come in at around the top 20-30 per year in my age group (40-45) in Norway; a little higher on the track, where fewer people compete.

My training has varied over the years, but for the past few years has been rather simple: reasonably high mileage, almost entirely at an easy effort, with occasional harder efforts (perhaps once every two to three weeks if not racing).

A piece of music from our wedding

The wonderful organ piece 'Marche Nuptiale' from 'Conte d'avril' by Widor was played at our wedding (in the chapel of University College, Oxford) during the signing of the register, where, with its tender beginning, it went down very well. I had stumbled upon it sometime previously; it is not very well-known, and I think that the recording I heard at that time is no longer readily available, but fortunately this one is great too! A little information on the piece can be found here.

The organist did not know the piece, and I ended up ordering the sheet music for him from a somewhat obscure shop in London specialising in a niche of it; we still have it!

Opera

Un'aura amorosa

Of Mozart's operas, Così fan tutte is my favourite; it is the energy of its ensemble and 'linking' pieces that make it for me, but I am also extremely fond of the tenor aria Un'aura amorosa that occurs towards the middle. By some distance, my favourite singer of it is Anton Dermota; he is able to make it flow like nobody else I have heard, with greatly expressive dynamics. His version can be heard here, taken from a wonderful 1955 performance conducted by Karl Böhm (though without an audience, it sounds like it is recorded live on a stage).

The Papageno-Papagena duet

I am extremely fond of the performance of the wonderful duet between Papageno and Papagena in Die Zauberflöte by Manfred Hemm and Barbara Kilduff at the Metropolitan Opera's production of this opera in 1991. A slightly longer clip can be viewed here. It is the staging and acting that makes the performance, particularly Kilduff's movement throughout in ballet across the stage; the brilliance of the piece lies partly in the wide range of emotions that are touched upon in such a short time, and the pair's expression of the nuances of this are authentic. An epithet of genius would I think be likely for anybody who could turn 48 (by my count!) consecutive "pa"'s into beautiful, evocative music! The short part of the duet section which is in a high register for both singers, beginning with the fourth "Kinderlein", is one of my favourite pieces of music; rare and true in its representation of bliss.
